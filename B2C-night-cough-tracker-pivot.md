# A bedside cough log for families

## The pitch

When your child has a cough that keeps coming back, you want to know whether it’s easing or happening more often. Once everyone is asleep, it’s hard to tell. We’re building a bedside cough log that gives families a record they can check in the morning and share at an appointment.

The box would process sound on the device and save counts without keeping recordings. In the morning, you’d see how many coughs it counted, when they clustered, and how the night compared with earlier nights. You could add a note if you noticed your child wake up or heard a wet cough during the day. A one-page summary would bring those observations together.

The research supports measuring change. In one hospital study, cough counts fell as patients recovered from pneumonia. In a separate study of 148 children, cough frequency and sound overlapped across common respiratory illnesses, including asthma, bronchiolitis, and pneumonia. We’re building the bedside log to give you a clearer record of how your child’s cough changes from night to night. [Boesch et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10152266/) · [Bisballe-Müller et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2020.06.067)

## What the evidence supports

- **Compare nights within the same child.** Cough counts changed over time in studies of acute respiratory illness and pneumonia. In a separate asthma study, both the asthma and healthy groups coughed less overnight, and their 24-hour counts overlapped. A bedroom tracker measures the overnight window. Its usefulness for tracking children at home still needs testing. [Acute cough study](https://doi.org/10.1183/09031936.00190111) · [Pneumonia study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10152266/) · [Asthma study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6619487/)
- **Keep the count and the family’s observations visible.** A cough cluster can contain several individual coughs. The report would show individual cough counts and when they occurred, alongside any waking a caregiver noticed. In chronic cough research, counts tracked perceived severity and disruption only weakly to moderately. A lab study also found that microphone placement affected measured sound power. [Cough counting study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10638680/) · [Cough burden study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9675653/) · [Cough sound study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5501240/)
- **Let families document how long a wet cough has been present.** A wet cough lasting more than four weeks in a child can be a clue to protracted bacterial bronchitis when other clinical criteria fit. A dated caregiver note can capture observations from across the day. The pediatric study evaluated wet and dry sounds from recordings, while automated wet/dry labeling in a child’s bedroom would need its own validation. [European Respiratory Society guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6942543/) · [Pediatric cough study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2020.06.067)

## What to test first

Start with families who want to track a child’s recurring night cough. That’s the proposed first audience. The research establishes that cough frequency can change during illness, while demand for a bedside product and the value of its report are open questions.

1. **Check the counts in real bedrooms.** Compare the prototype with manually annotated recordings collected with consent during a pilot. Measure missed coughs and false counts, especially on quiet nights and when someone else coughs nearby.
2. **See whether families can use the summary.** Ask caregivers to compare several nights and add context like observed waking or daytime cough. Ask clinicians what, if anything, the summary adds to a visit.
3. **Test willingness to pay.** Compare interest in buying the box with borrowing it for a few weeks before an appointment.
