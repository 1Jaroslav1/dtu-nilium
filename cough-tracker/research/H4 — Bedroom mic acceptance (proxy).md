# H4 — Bedroom microphone acceptance (desk proxy)

**Summary:** Desk **proxy** for H4. No verdict. A wearable cough monitor with privacy-limited audio scored >80% favourable acceptability (n=54). No qualitative study of attitudes to a bedroom microphone was found, so we're relying on interviews.

## Hypothesis

> Patients accept a microphone in the bedroom if no audio leaves the device.

**Type:** conversation (H#), proxy only.

## Evidence (proxy)

| Claim | Value | Tier | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-mo wearable acceptability | >80% favourable across all questionnaire domains (n=54); audio recorded only 17.7% of the time | A | [DATA] | ERJ Open Research 2026 |
| Unobtrusive monitors | One prior study: perceived as burden-free, no participant concerns | B (cited in review) | [DATA] | MDPI 2024 review |
| Albus Home in bedrooms | Validated in real bedrooms; attitudes **not reported** | A | gap | PMC9720547 |
| Qualitative bedroom-audio study | **None found** | — | gap | — |

## Proxy finding

Study participants accept privacy-limited audio monitoring. But they're volunteers, which inflates acceptance, and they used a wearable, not a box in the bedroom. **H4 stays open.** The "no audio leaves the device" claim is also exactly what Hyfe and C-mo already advertise, so it's table stakes, not a differentiator.

## Interview question (past behaviour)

- "Has anyone ever recorded you or your child sleeping, for a doctor or an app? What happened?" (already in 02, block 4)
