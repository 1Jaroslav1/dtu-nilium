# H1 — Who acts on a cough count (desk proxy)

**Summary:** Desk **proxy** for H1. No verdict, because H1 is a conversation hypothesis. The literature says objective cough counts are **rarely used outside research**, held back by cost, validation effort and unproven clinical utility. In care homes, cough is a weak pneumonia signal; respiratory rate is a much stronger one.

## Hypothesis

> At least one segment already makes a decision based on how much someone coughs.

**Type:** conversation (H#), proxy only. Interviews decide it.

## What the desk can and can't tell us

It can tell us whether the *field as a whole* acts on cough counts. It can't tell us whether *our interviewees* do, or would.

## Proxy finding

**Today, the only group that clearly makes decisions on a cough count is drug-trial sponsors,** and incumbents own that market. Clinics mostly don't act on counts, because utility hasn't been shown and the tools are costly. In care homes, staff act on fever, function decline, respiratory rate and SpO₂, not on cough frequency.

**Implication:** our interviews should try hard to *disprove* this proxy. If 10 conversations confirm it, H1 fails and the gate goes yellow.

## Sharper interview questions (added to 02)

- Clinic: "Tell me about the last patient whose cough you tried to quantify. What did you use, and did the number change what you did?"
- Care home: "Think of the last resident who developed a chest infection. What was the first thing anyone noticed, and at what time of day?"

## New hypotheses spotted (not researched)

- **S6 (desk):** respiratory rate during sleep is the better care-home signal. Can the kit measure it? The 8×8 distance sensor (VL53L5CX) might see chest movement, or the mic might pick up breathing sounds. Untested.
