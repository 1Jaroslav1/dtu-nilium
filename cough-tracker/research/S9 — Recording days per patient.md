# S9 — Recording days per patient

**Summary:** Desk research on S9. Verdict: **confirmed**. Registered chronic-cough drug trials record **one 24 h session per visit** (baseline, then e.g. weeks 4, 8 and 12), not consecutive nights.

**Last Updated:** 2026-11-21

## Hypothesis

> Studies record only 1–2 days per patient per time point, not multi-night.

## Pre-committed criteria

| | Criterion | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation | ≥2 registered protocols using single 24 h sessions per visit | Combined with S5 variability, the "snapshot is noisy" problem holds |
| Refutation | Protocols commonly use ≥3 consecutive days per time point | Measurement noise is already handled, so no problem to solve |

## Evidence

| Claim | Value | Tier | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gefapixant MK-7264-012 | 24 h recordings at baseline (day −1), weeks 4, 8, 12, follow-up day 98 | A | [DATA] | NCT02612610 |
| Gefapixant COUGH-1 | 24 h recording; ≥20 h counted as valid; week-12 / baseline ratio | A | [DATA] | NCT03449134 |
| Eliapixant PAGANINI | 24 h VitaloJAK recording at each visit to week 12 | A | [DATA] | NCT04562155 (via Lung 2023) |
| Counter-example | Hyfe-based studies run continuous multi-day monitoring | A | [DATA] | [[S7 — Research tier already exists]] |

## Verdict

**confirmed**, for pharma registration trials.

**Why:** three registered protocols use single 24 h sessions per visit [DATA].

**What it changes:** with S5 (~38% day-to-day variation), the measurement-quality problem is real in trials. But the solution, continuous multi-day monitoring, is already offered by Hyfe (S7), so the gap is in practice, not in available technology.
