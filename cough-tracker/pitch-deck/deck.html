One day at the clinic

Kasia has been coughing for months.

New medicines are coming. To prove they work, doctors count coughs.

Kasia wears a recorder for one day at the clinic.

That day, Kasia hardly coughs.

Illustration 02: clinic, Kasia wired up and fine, Professor waiting, Pucio watching the clock

New cough drugs are judged by an objective count of coughs per hour, usually from 24 hours of recording at a few clinic visits.

Which day is real?

On Monday, Kasia coughs all evening.

On Tuesday, almost not at all.

The Professor only has Tuesday. He frowns at the chart.

Pucio holds up a calendar: "Which day is the real Kasia?"

Illustration 04: Pucio shows the calendar, a busy Monday and an empty Tuesday

A person's cough changes about 38% from one day to the next. In two out of three people, a single day doesn't show their real pattern.

4

The expensive box

The Professor wants many more days.

Artur unpacks the monitor: €11,000.

Each recording needs 1.5 hours of listening.

Pucio gets a questionnaire: "Cough: a little or a lot?"

Illustration 05: an expensive monitor, an empty wallet, Pucio with a two-box questionnaire

Gold-standard monitors cost ≈ €11,000 each plus ~1.5 h of analyst time per recording. Academic groups, including every Danish group we found, use questionnaires instead of counts.

Nothing to wear

Artur draws a vest with 37 sensors.

Kasia shakes her head: "I won't sleep in that."

Pucio walks to the bed.

Pucio puts a small box on the nightstand.

Illustration 07: Artur's 37-sensor vest vs Pucio's small box on the nightstand

A bedside smart-home assistant that counts coughs automatically every night, with nothing to wear and no audio stored.

Every night, at home

Kasia sleeps in her own bed. The box counts every cough.

The air gets dry, so the box switches on the humidifier.

In the morning, the Professor sees every night, not just one day.

Artur reaches for the confetti. Pucio hides it: "First, the trial."

Illustration 08: night (device + humidifier) / morning (many nightly bars, confetti hidden)

Researchers get objective multi-night data from the patient's real home. Patients get a device that helps their bedroom instead of just watching them.
