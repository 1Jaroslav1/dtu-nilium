# Cough type and diagnostic accuracy

## What do wet, dry, and nighttime cough indicate?

### Takeaway

Wet and dry cough can be distinguished reproducibly in recordings, and wet cough can indicate airway mucus. The distinction has much weaker power to identify the cause of an acute illness. Persistent wet cough carries more weight in children, and isolated nighttime cough carries little diagnostic weight for asthma.

### Cited Findings

- **Bisballe-Müller et al., CHEST (2021)** is the most directly applicable study. This prospective hospital cohort included 118 children with asthma, bronchiolitis, pneumonia, or other acute respiratory infections, plus 30 controls. Cases had 24-hour recordings of spontaneous cough. Across illnesses, wet cough occurred in 42–63%. Blinded listeners agreed closely when labeling wet versus dry (Cohen’s κ 0.89, 95% CI 0.81–0.97), yet specialist diagnoses based on cough recordings agreed only slightly with the hospital discharge diagnosis (κ 0.13, 95% CI 0.03–0.22). Day/night frequency, sound, and wet/dry type did not reliably distinguish the common acute illnesses. Counts correlated moderately with caregiver visual analogue scores (Spearman r=0.43, 95% CI 0.25–0.56). Cases were hospitalized and the median age was 2.0 years. Controls supplied voluntary coughs, so avoid applying case–control count comparisons. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2020.06.067), [full abstract describing population and findings](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0012369220318699).
- **Chang et al., Cough (2005)** compared clinician and parent classifications against bronchoscopic findings in children scheduled for bronchoscopy. Clinician-assessed wet cough yielded 75% sensitivity and 79% specificity for the study’s threshold of airway secretions (grade 4). Dry cough could still occur with mild secretions. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15638942/).
- **Marchant et al., Thorax (2006)** studied 100 referred children (median age 2.8) whose cough lasted >3 weeks under that study’s then-current definition. A clinician-observed moist cough was associated with identifying a specific underlying cause (OR 9.34, 95% CI 3.49–25.03). This is an association within a selected tertiary cohort and the outcome included multiple conditions. **Marchant et al., Thorax (2012)** randomized children with chronic wet cough, with resolution in 48% on amoxicillin/clavulanate and 16% on placebo. Modern ERS guidance treats continuous wet cough for >4 weeks without pointers to other causes and resolving after appropriate treatment as criteria for clinical protracted bacterial bronchitis (PBB). Other causes to consider include bronchiectasis and aspiration. [2006 prospective study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2104702/), [2012 randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22628120/), [ERS guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6942543/).
- **Altiner et al., Scandinavian Journal of Primary Health Care (2009)** studied 241 adults with acute cough and no underlying chronic lung disease, 28 of whom met a microbiological definition of bacterial infection. Yellow/green sputum had 79% sensitivity, 46% specificity and positive likelihood ratio of 1.46 (95% CI 1.17–1.85). This is a weak association and provides little diagnostic information alone. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19242860/), [publisher full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/02813430902759663).
- **Jurca et al., ERJ Open Research (2020)** used a population-based Leicester respiratory cohort (4,101 children at age one, 2,854 at age four, 2,369 at age six, and 1,688 at age nine). Isolated nighttime cough generally did not predict subsequent wheeze more often than in asymptomatic children, except at age one (17% with isolated night cough versus 9% asymptomatic reported wheeze three years later). GINA recognizes cough-variant asthma and describes airway hyperresponsiveness on bronchial challenge as a key finding. These sources support considering asthma in context, including associated wheeze, variable symptoms, and lung testing. [Leicester cohort](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erjor/6/4/00217-2020), [GINA summary guide](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf).

### Inferences

- A reproducible wet/dry label is an airway-secretion clue. Duration, age, and associated signs determine its clinical meaning.
- A chronic wet cough pathway in a child differs from the inference that an acute productive cough or green phlegm identifies bacteria.

### Gaps

- Longitudinal validation of specific cough types across unselected community populations was limited in the evidence reviewed.

## What can unusual patterns or accompanying symptoms tell us?

### Takeaway

Barking cough with hoarseness and inspiratory stridor is a classic clinical cluster for croup in young children. A whoop after coughing or vomiting triggered by a fit raises suspicion for pertussis, with limited accuracy from each sign alone. Pneumonia and influenza have substantial symptom overlap with competing diagnoses.

### Cited Findings

- **Miyashita et al., BMC Infectious Diseases (2013)** compared 183 lab-confirmed adolescent/adult pertussis cases with 1,132 patients without lab confirmation. Coughing fits were 90% sensitive and 25% specific. A whoop was 19% sensitive and 86% specific. Vomiting after coughing was 25% sensitive and 80% specific. In the **CHEST guideline and meta-analysis** adult pooled estimates were 93.2% sensitivity and 20.6% specificity for coughing fits, 29.8% and 79.5% for a whoop, and 32.5% and 77.7% for vomiting after coughing. A whoop shifted the odds modestly (positive likelihood ratio 1.46). In children vomiting after coughing was 60% sensitive and 66% specific. Infants sometimes present with breathing pauses and little or no cough, and prior vaccination can change the presentation. [Miyashita et al. primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3600016/), [CHEST pooled estimates table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6859243/#tbl5), [CDC clinical features](https://www.cdc.gov/pertussis/hcp/clinical-signs/index.html).
- **Canadian Paediatric Society guideline** describes rapid onset of seal-like bark, hoarse voice and stridor (a harsh sound while breathing in) as classic for croup, typically due to viral inflammation of the upper airway. Multiple viruses cause the syndrome, including parainfluenza and SARS-CoV-2, so the clinical cluster identifies croup without determining the virus. [Croup guideline](https://cps.ca/en/documents/position/acute-management-of-croup).
- **van Vugt et al., BMJ (2013)** prospectively assessed 2,820 adults with acute cough in European primary care. Only 140 (5%) had radiographic pneumonia. History/exam features with independent predictive value included breathlessness, crackles, reduced breath sounds, fever, tachycardia and absence of runny nose (model AUC 0.70), improving to 0.77 with CRP above 30 mg/L. Acute cough presence was the entry criterion and the model did not rely on any specific cough sound. [Primary diagnostic study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3639712/).
- **Ebell et al., BMJ Open (2025)** synthesized 67 prospective studies selected for a high-quality reference standard. Across age groups, any cough was 92% sensitive and 22% specific for influenza. The positive likelihood ratio was 1.2 overall and the negative likelihood ratio was 0.28 in adults. The authors found limited diagnostic accuracy for symptoms or case definitions. This evidence addresses presence of cough and symptom combinations. The presence of cough and fever only modestly identified influenza. [Diagnostic accuracy meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40032401/), [full paper with estimates](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/bmjopen/15/3/e067574.full.pdf).

### Inferences

- For pertussis, rates that are highly sensitive or relatively specific can still leave substantial uncertainty. Age, vaccination, timing, and whether there was exposure are relevant.
- Croup is a clinical syndrome, so recognizing the combined pattern and identifying the virus are separate questions.

### Gaps

- No validated sensitivity or specificity estimate for bark alone was found in the guideline.
- Studies above do not establish a unique human-audible pneumonia or influenza cough.

## Do acoustic tests classify disease reliably in new populations?

### Takeaway

Algorithms can recognize cough events or measurable features. Disease identification needs a distinct clinical validation step. Several larger or external validations show that apparently promising results can weaken under more realistic comparisons.

### Cited Findings

- **Coppock et al., Nature Machine Intelligence (2024)** linked respiratory audio to PCR outcomes in 67,842 people, including 23,514 positives. Unadjusted ROC-AUC was 0.846, falling to 0.619 after matching on age, sex/gender, and reported symptoms. Symptom-based models performed better in practical settings and external testing. This study used multiple respiratory audio modalities, so describe it as respiratory audio including cough. [Original paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-023-00773-8), [Oxford open repository](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A0a9eaf2f-b926-4e05-afb7-09aad54d1a96).
- **Zimmer et al., Scientific Reports (2026)** independently evaluated 11 tuberculosis screening algorithms in 303 adults with cough recruited in Lima. Models previously achieved AUROCs 0.689–0.743 within the larger CODA Challenge evaluation set. In the Peruvian external data AUROCs fell to 0.480–0.615. [Original external validation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-50492-4), [open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13462625/).
- **Porter et al., Respiratory Research (2019)** tested an algorithm in 585 children aged 29 days to 12 years. Using the first five recorded coughs plus up to five symptom inputs, positive/negative agreement with an expert clinical panel was 97%/91% for asthma/RAD, 87%/85% for pneumonia, 85%/82% for croup, and 84%/81% for bronchiolitis. The panel is a clinical comparator and listened to cough audio when it suspected croup, the tool includes symptoms, and several authors had financial interests. It does not establish audio-only diagnosis or broad generalizability. [Full primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6551890/), [PubMed conflict statement and results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31167662/).
- **Xu et al., European Respiratory Review (2026)** systematically assessed 89 acoustic studies from 34 countries (31 detection, 55 disease classification, 3 disease severity prediction). Just 11.2% used external validation. Across assessed outcomes, 71.1–87.6% were high risk of bias according to PROBAST-AI. [Systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42342266/).
- **Smith et al., Cough (2006)** tested audio-only human interpretation in a small study. Fifty-three health professionals listened to nine recordings of spontaneous cough from patients with various established diagnoses. They recognized whether there was mucus 76.1% of the time and got the underlying diagnosis right 34.0% of the time. The recordings were few, so use alongside Bisballe-Müller rather than treating the percentage as community performance. [Original experiment](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1745-9974-2-1).
- **Lee et al., BMJ Open Respiratory Research (2017)** found strong median within-person correlations between recorded cough power/energy from voluntary cough and physical strength in a 32-person study (airflow r=0.87–0.88, pressure r=0.89), with highly repeatable results at standardized placement. Changing microphone position changed measured power. The paper validates measuring cough strength under controlled conditions, not identifying disease or illness severity. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28725446/), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5501240/).

### Inferences

- Distinguishing cough from background, labeling wet versus dry, estimating effort and identifying an underlying illness are four different test tasks. Performance on one does not transfer to another.
- A classifier’s ability to separate patients from healthy people or a particular recruitment group can overestimate its ability to separate a specific infection from other illnesses among symptomatic patients.

### Gaps

- Some future context-specific clinical models may perform adequately, but the available external validation cannot support a generalized sound-only diagnostic claim. Independent evaluation should compare against realistic competing illnesses at different sites and against simpler symptom models.
