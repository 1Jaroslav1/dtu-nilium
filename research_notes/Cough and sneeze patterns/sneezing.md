# What sneeze patterns tell us

## What do count, intensity and sneeze type measure?

### Takeaway

Counting sneezes can measure response to known triggers. A symptom severity rating measures how bothersome sneezing feels. Studies that report the percentage of cases with sneezing measure its presence. These quantities answer different questions.

### Cited Findings

- Hansen and Mygind (2002, *Rhinology*) had 80 Copenhagen hospital employees and trainees who considered themselves free of rhinitis record sneezes for 14 days. Participants averaged 1.2 sneezes a day, and more than 95% averaged fewer than four per day. It was a convenience sample with self-reported absence of rhinitis, studied in summer, and included no comparison group of confirmed allergy or infection. The four-per-day figure is an observation in this sample, with no validated diagnostic sensitivity/specificity. [Full paper](https://www.rhinologyjournal.com/Rhinology_issues/113.pdf)
- Doyle et al. (1995, *Ann Allergy Asthma Immunol*) counted sneezes in 18 adults with known ragweed allergy during two single-blind randomized intranasal allergen sessions and one placebo session outside the ragweed season. Average dose–response curves were reproducible between allergen sessions and different from placebo. The controlled exposure and within-person reproducibility are what make the count informative. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7697479/)
- A controlled house-dust-mite challenge scored sneezing as fewer than 3, 3–5, or over 5 sneezes over a specific time after challenge. A different allergen provocation trial scored 0, ≤5, 6–10 and >10. These are protocol-defined endpoints. [Comparative house-dust-mite challenge study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3104480/) [Nasal corticosteroid/allergen exposure trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4338223/)
- Michalak et al. (2020, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*) tested listeners identifying recorded coughs and sneezes as illness-associated or not. In Study 1 accuracy averaged 45%, consistent with 50% chance. Illness sources self-reported that they had an infectious illness. The study addresses human auditory judgment, with no lab confirmation or assessment of automated classifiers. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7341931/)

### Inferences

- A temporary rise in counts is compatible with a stimulus response. Its meaning depends on the trigger, baseline, time window, and accompanying symptoms. [Hansen and Mygind](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12012947/) [Doyle et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7697479/)

### Gaps

- The search did not identify a clinical validation of loudness, airflow/force, wet versus dry sneeze, or single sneeze versus number in a burst as independently reliable for sorting allergy, irritant rhinitis and lab-confirmed infections. Paroxysms alone are nonspecific. Rare intractable paroxysms have been described in case reports with other causes, which are not representative of usual bouts. [Lin et al. case report series](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14700443/)

## What points toward allergic rather than nonallergic rhinitis?

### Takeaway

Recurrent sneezing with itchy nose or eyes, watery eyes/runny nose, and a recognizable or seasonal trigger is suggestive. There is overlap with other types of rhinitis, and the clinical history plus evidence of sensitization to a plausible trigger is more informative.

### Cited Findings

- Di Lorenzo et al. (2011) studied 1,511 adults with rhinitis who underwent allergy evaluation including skin-prick testing. Sneezing was associated with allergic rhinitis versus nonallergic rhinitis in the adjusted model (OR 4.09, 95% CI 2.78–6.00). Nasal itching (OR 3.84) and conjunctivitis (OR 4.49) were also informative. This comparison was across rhinitis groups, and the reported statistic is not a diagnostic probability. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1159/000320050)
- Zhu et al. (2026) prospectively enrolled 1,150 patients with suspected allergic rhinitis in one hospital, excluded acute illnesses, and used skin-prick testing and concordant symptoms in the reference diagnosis. A self-rated 0–10 sneezing-burden score gave an AUC of 0.758 in training and 0.731 in the internal holdout. The full symptom model had an AUC of 0.765 in the holdout according to Table 2 and the abstract. The rating measured impact on daily life. This was an allergy versus nonallergic rhinitis comparison, with no validation for allergy versus acute cold/flu. Because characteristic symptoms entered the reference diagnosis, some incorporation bias is possible. Table 2 labels sensitivity and specificity but gives numbers inconsistent with the adjacent confusion counts, so prefer AUC. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1002/wjo2.70109)
- Wedbäck et al. (2005) compared 17 people with seasonal nonallergic rhinitis, 20 with seasonal allergic rhinitis, and 13 with persistent nonallergic rhinitis using skin and nasal provocation tests, symptoms and other measures. Sneezing occurred more often in both seasonal groups than in persistent nonallergic rhinitis. Eye symptoms were more prominent in the seasonal allergic group. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16008061/)
- Luyten et al. (2024, EPOCHAL) tracked 396 adults and found the strongest association between relevant pollen exposure and self-rated composite nasal/eye symptoms in the five hours before symptom scoring. This is evidence for timing with exposure, using overall symptoms rather than per-day sneeze counts. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1111/all.16130)
- The 2023 International Consensus Statement describes sneezing with nasal/ocular itch, a clear allergen trigger, and recurrence in the same season as suggestive of allergic rhinitis. The 2020 US practice parameter reports that sneezing is common in acute upper respiratory infection and can occur in nonallergic rhinitis, and that cold dry air, odors, tobacco smoke and other irritants can trigger nonallergic rhinitis. [ICAR 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/alr.23090) [Rhinitis 2020](https://www.aaaai.org/Aaaai/media/MediaLibrary/PDF%20Documents/Practice%20and%20Parameters/Rhinitis-2020-A-practice-parameter-update.pdf)

### Inferences

- The combination of timing with exposure plus itching is stronger evidence than a specific single-sneeze pattern. Even seasonality can occur in seasonal nonallergic rhinitis. [ICAR 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/alr.23090) [Wedbäck et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16008061/)

### Gaps

- The available allergy clinic and challenge studies do not establish a transferable sneeze count that distinguishes allergy from acute infection in unselected people.

## What does sneezing tell us about specific respiratory infections?

### Takeaway

Sneezing occurs with many lab-confirmed respiratory viruses. The proportion of people who sneeze differs by pathogen and study. The symptom has considerable overlap across rhinovirus, endemic coronaviruses, influenza, RSV and SARS-CoV-2.

### Cited Findings

- Geismar et al. (2023, *Scientific Reports*) compared 11,766 confirmed illnesses in English and Welsh household cohorts. In Table 1, sneezing was reported in 90% of rhinovirus cases (n=283), 90% of endemic seasonal coronavirus cases (n=191), 77% RSV (n=84), 76% influenza (n=222), and 49%, 60%, and 55% of the Omicron BA.1, BA.2 and BA.5 periods, respectively. Measures are percentages of illnesses where sneezing was reported, not sneeze events per time. Flu Watch (2006–2011) and Virus Watch (2020–2022) had different enrollment/testing criteria and age distributions, and COVID variants were assigned by regional dominance rather than individual sequencing. Cough also overlapped, ranging from 79–91% across the four non-COVID pathogens. [Study, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10397315/#Tab1)
- Rao et al. (1995) compared day-by-day symptoms in 139 natural colds of confirmed rhinovirus etiology with 125 experimental rhinovirus colds. Sneezing prevalence differed on days 1–2 between the datasets, though the illnesses were broadly similar. [Study](https://doi.org/10.2500/105065895781874114)
- Research on variant-specific symptoms in 1.54 million adults in England found that sneezing was among cold-like symptoms contributing to prediction of PCR positivity in Omicron periods, and its odds differed even between BA.1 and BA.2. The result concerns association with PCR positivity in a setting and time, not validation of sneezing as a test. [REACT-1 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34244-2)

### Inferences

- The same symptom can have useful population-level associations and weak individual-level discrimination. In Geismar et al., rhinovirus and endemic coronaviruses had identical reported sneezing rates, and influenza and RSV were virtually identical. [Study, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10397315/#Tab1)

### Gaps

- Same-cohort studies measuring counts per hour/day, intensity and phenotype against simultaneously assessed allergic, nonallergic and pathogen-confirmed groups are missing from the findings above.
