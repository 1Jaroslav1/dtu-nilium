# Story rules

## Voice

- Picture-book simple. Short sentences, present tense, concrete nouns. A 6-year-old could follow the story lines, and an investor could quote the moral.
- Show, don't explain. "Kasia has three minutes before the meeting." beats "Customers are time-constrained."
- Deadpan humour. The joke is the gap between what the founder believes and what Pucio sees. No puns, no winking at the audience, no insider jargon.
- Use the founder's flaw kindly. The founder is lovable and learns. They are never the villain.
- Pucio never lectures. He points, asks one naive question, or does one small action (hides the confetti, holds up the right photo).

## The page formula

| Part | Rule | Example pattern (from the reference book, paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| Heading | 2–4 words, a plain business term | "Who uses, who decides?" |
| Story line 1 | Set the scene, one fact | "Kasia will use it. Her boss will pay." |
| Story line 2 | The founder acts on his flaw | "Artur has been emailing the intern for a week." |
| Story line 3 *(optional)* | A consequence | |
| **Beat** | Bold. The twist, Pucio's line or action | "**The intern only wanted the ebook.**" |
| Illustration | One moment, showing exactly the beat | A red string tying user → approver → payer |
| Moral | 1–2 lines, the business lesson stated plainly | "Find out who uses the product, who approves the purchase and who pays." |

Length budget per story page: ≤ 40 words of story + beat, and ≤ 25 words of moral.

## Beat-line types (rotate them, don't repeat one type twice in a row)

1. **Pucio's question:** a naive question that exposes the flaw. ("Do you read this or install it?")
2. **Pucio's gesture:** he points at the truth. ("Pucio points at the clock.")
3. **The founder's overshoot:** the founder jumps ahead. ("Artur is already ordering confetti.")
4. **The cold fact:** a flat statement that lands. ("The customers don't know.")
5. **The quiet fix:** Pucio quietly sets it right. ("Pucio hid the confetti.")

## Problem pages (2)

- **Page A, the pain:** the customer in a concrete, visible struggle. The founder isn't there yet, or is busy with the wrong thing. Moral = the problem in one sentence, quotable.
- **Page B, why it stays unsolved:** today's workaround fails, or the cost is shown (the pile of papers, the clock, the wallet). Moral = the cost or consequence, with one number if you have it ("…which takes 6 hours a week").

## Solution pages (2)

- **Page A, the turn:** Pucio's truth changes how the founder sees it, and the product appears as one simple object on screen or in hand. Moral = what the product is (form factor) and what it does, in one line.
- **Page B, the outcome:** the customer after, with the same visual struggle now resolved (the clock no longer matters, the laptop is unlocked). Moral = the result, how fast, how little effort.

Never list features on story pages. If you want to show features, make it a joke (the endless paper roll of 37 features) and put the real list on the Tools/Tech notebook page.

## Team page (1)

- Artur stands in for the founder. Co-founders are drawn next to him in the same storybook style, together with Pucio, and they count toward the 5-character limit.
- Story lines: what each of them did that makes them right for this. Use real, verifiable facts told simply ("Artur (in real life: [founder name]) built hospital software for 8 years.").
- Beat: Pucio's verdict, or the running gag (Pucio hands them the confetti tube: "Not yet. After the first customer.").
- Moral: "Why us" in one line.

## Cover and closing

- **Cover:** the title "PUCIO [verb] [thing]" in big red caps style, a subtitle "Pucio and Artur [mission]" (as in the example: "Pucio and Artur go to market"), and an illustration of the cast with the running gag object visible. No moral.
- **Closing:** a quiet scene (the cast at the table, a notebook open), with the gag resolved or deliberately left open, and one call-to-action line ("Shall we keep going?", "Who's in?"). Can carry the logo and contact.

## Worked mini-example (cough tracker, English)

Cast (the storybook people, see `cast.md`): Pucio · Artur (founder, wants to measure everything; his T-shirt print says the app name) · Kasia (here: a mum whose son coughs at night) · gag: the confetti tube.

**Problem A, "Night shift"**
Kasia's son coughs all night. In the morning the doctor asks: "How often?" Kasia guesses.
**Pucio holds up three fingers, then five.**
Moral: *Parents have no objective record of night-time cough, so doctors decide on a guess.*

**Solution A, "The turn"**
Artur shows 37 charts. Kasia looks at the clock. Pucio taps just one number.
**Artur deletes 36 charts.**
Moral: *An app that counts cough overnight and gives the doctor a one-page summary.*

**Solution B, "The morning after"**
The phone counted every cough. Kasia shows the doctor one page. It takes ten seconds.
**Pucio finally hands Artur the confetti.**
Moral: *The doctor decides on data, not a guess, in 10 seconds and with zero effort from the parent.*
